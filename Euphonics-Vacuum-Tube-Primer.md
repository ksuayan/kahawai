# Euphonics: A Vacuum-Tube Primer

*Why glowing glass sounds the way it does, which tubes earned their legends,
and what any of that has to do with a DSP slider.*

---

## What "euphonic" actually means

*Euphonic* literally means "pleasant-sounding" — which makes it the rare
audiophile term that admits what it is: a preference, not a measurement.
When people say a tube amplifier sounds "euphonic," they're describing a
particular *flavor* of imperfection, one that human hearing happens to find
agreeable.

The core of it is harmonic distortion — overtones the amplifier adds that
weren't in the recording. A single-ended triode amplifier produces mostly
**second-harmonic** distortion: energy at exactly twice the fundamental
frequency, i.e. one octave up. An octave is the most consonant interval in
music; the ear reads it as fullness, body, warmth. A push-pull design
cancels even-order harmonics and leaves **odd-order** products (third,
fifth) — intervals that grate, which is why a clipping transistor amp
sounds harsh while an overdriven tube amp sounds, well, like rock and roll.

None of this is magic, and none of it is accuracy. A euphonic amplifier is
*less* faithful to the signal by every bench measurement. The honest
position — the only one worth holding — is that some listeners prefer the
beautiful lie. This primer is about the lie's ingredients.

## A short history of the glowing bottle

**1904–1907: The valve and the Audion.** John Ambrose Fleming patents the
thermionic diode; Lee de Forest adds a third electrode — the grid — and the
Audion triode can *amplify*. Every tube amplifier since is a refinement of
that 1906 idea.

**1920s–30s: Radio and the movies.** RCA standardizes receiving tubes
(201A, 45, 2A3 — the **RCA 2A3** of 1933 remains a directly-heated triode
legend). In 1938, Western Electric introduces the **300B**, a power triode
built to amplify telephone signals, descended from the 1933 300A developed
at Bell Labs. It hums away behind cinema
curtains for decades; nobody yet suspects it will become the most
mythologized audio tube ever made.

**1947: The Williamson.** D.T.N. Williamson publishes his amplifier design
in *Wireless World* — push-pull **KT66**s, triode-wired, with deep feedback.
It becomes the template for serious hi-fi and proves tubes can be both
powerful and *measured* clean.

**The 1950s: the golden age.** The catalog of legends is written in about
a decade: the **12AX7** (RCA, 1947) becomes the universal preamp tube; the
**6L6** beam tetrode (RCA, 1936) powers Fender's tweed era; Mullard
introduces the **EL34** in late 1953 — a true pentode that becomes the
voice of British amplification; Tung-Sol
releases the **6550**; GEC's Marconi-Osram division produces the **KT88**
(1956), the "kinkless tetrode," and the EL34-compatible **KT77** (1958);
Amperex introduces the **6DJ8/ECC88** (1958), a frame-grid triode built for
VHF tuners that audiophiles will later adopt as a line-stage darling.

**1960s: peak and plateau.** Mullard (Blackburn), Telefunken (Ulm),
Amperex (Heerlen), RCA, Tung-Sol, Sylvania, GE — the great factories run
full tilt. The tubes made in this decade, especially the early 1960s, are
the ones collectors now call "new old stock" and pay accordingly.

**1970s–80s: the eclipse.** Transistors win on cost, size, heat, and —
crucially — measured distortion. Tube production collapses. In 1988,
Western Electric manufactures its last 300B.

**1990s–2000s: the revival.** Audiophiles rediscover single-ended triodes;
guitar culture never left. Westrex restarts limited 300B production
(1997–~2009). New factories rise: **JJ Electronic** (Slovakia), **New
Sensor** (Russia — Sovtek, Electro-Harmonix, and reissues of the Tung-Sol,
Mullard, and Genalex Gold Lion names), **Shuguang/Psvane** (China), and
boutique makers like **Emission Labs**, **KR Audio**, and **Takatsuki**.

**2018–present: the homecoming.** Western Electric establishes the
Rossville Works in Georgia and resumes the 300B on the original 1938
standards — first new batches shipping late 2020 — with cathode material
traced to a 1963 Hawthorne Works melt. In
2022, with Russian supply sanctioned and Chinese production shrinking, WE
announces it will build guitar-amp staples — 12AX7, 6L6, EL34, EL84, 6V6 —
in the USA, the 12AX7 first. The tube, it turns out, was never dead. It
was waiting.

## Anatomy of the tube sound

Before the shopping list, the physics — because this is the part a DSP has
to reckon with. "Tube sound" is several mechanisms stacked:

1. **Harmonic spectrum.** Single-ended circuits emphasize the 2nd harmonic
(warm, full); push-pull cancels evens and leaves odds (leaner, harder
when pushed). The *ratio* of harmonics matters more than the total
amount.
2. **Soft clipping.** Tubes saturate gradually — distortion rises gently
with level, compressing peaks before anything sounds "broken."
Transistors clip hard at the rail. This is most of the "forgiving"
reputation.
3. **The output transformer.** Nearly every tube power amp couples to the
speaker through iron. Transformers limit bandwidth (softening extremes),
add hysteresis coloration, and ring a little. Much of what people call
"tube warmth" is transformer behavior.
4. **High output impedance.** Tube amps (low or no feedback, high source
impedance) let the *speaker's* impedance curve shape the frequency
response — a coupling effect, not a property of the tube alone.
5. **Sag and microdynamics.** Tube rectifiers (below) drop voltage under
load; the amp breathes with the music. Subtle compression, felt more
than heard.
6. **Noise floor.** Honest caveat: hiss, hum, and microphonics are part of
the vintage package. Nobody models those on purpose.

## The small-signal tubes

These do the voltage amplification — the character of the preamp and input
stages. Small changes here are audible because everything downstream
multiplies them.

**12AX7 / ECC83** — the universal preamp tube (gain factor 100). The
legends, with their consensus descriptions:
- *Telefunken ECC83, smooth plate (West Germany)* — the detail champion:
extended, airy highs, precise imaging. The diamond mark on the base is
the tell.
- *Mullard ECC83, long plate (Blackburn, UK)* — rich, warm mids; the
"romantic" 12AX7. Early-60s long-plates are the prize.
- *Amperex "Bugle Boy" (Heerlen, Holland)* — lively and holographic, a
touch forward.
- *RCA black plate (USA)* — warm, musical, forgiving.
- *Tung-Sol (USA)* — dynamic and detailed; originals are scarce and priced
like it.
- *5751* — a lower-gain (70) military variant; GE 5-star and RCA versions
are prized for taming hot preamps.

**6SN7** — the great octal driver tube of the 1940s, still beloved in
modern designs. *Tung-Sol round plate* (the "tall boy") is the collector's
grail — open and refined. *RCA red base 5692* is lush and warm;
*Sylvania* is balanced and honest; *Ken-Rad black glass* is dark and
brooding. Entire preamps are designed around this tube.

**6DJ8 / ECC88 / 6922 family** — Amperex's 1958 frame-grid triode, adopted
by audiophiles for line stages and DAC outputs. The *Amperex Bugle Boy
6DJ8* (Holland, 1960s, "A-frame") is the legend — airy, spacious, vivid.
*Siemens ECC88* is cooler and more precise; *Telefunken ECC88* splits the
difference. The premium variants — *Amperex 7308 PQ* and especially the
*Siemens CCa* — are the "holy grail" tier, with prices to match.

**12AU7 / 12AT7** — lower-gain siblings of the 12AX7 (gain 20 and 60).
*RCA clear-top 12AU7*, *Mullard 12AU7*, *Telefunken 12AT7* all have their
devotees, mostly as drivers and phase splitters where their linearity
matters more than their flavor.

## The power tubes

These move the speaker. Their distortion character *is* the amplifier's
character at volume.

**The British pentodes:**
- *EL34 (Mullard, Blackburn)* — the "British" midrange: sweet, present,
harmonically rich. XF1/XF2 codes, early 1960s, are the benchmark. The
voice of the Marshall stack and countless hi-fi classics.
- *KT77 (GEC/MOV)* — a 1958 EL34-compatible beam tetrode with aligned
grids: lower distortion, greater reliability, similar soul. Scarce and
loved.

**The kinkless tetrodes (GEC/MOV):**
- *KT88* — the big refined one: powerful, extended, composed. The original
MOV/GEC bottles are the reference for "power with manners."
- *KT66* — the Williamson tube: warm, vintage, intimate at lower power.

**The American beam tubes:**
- *6550 (Tung-Sol)* — big American bass and headroom; original Tung-Sols
are legendary and priced accordingly.
- *6L6GC (RCA black plate)* — the tweed-Fender warmth machine; round,
bluesy, forgiving.
- *6V6 (RCA)* — the small sweet one: a few watts of pure charm.

**The directly-heated triodes (the SET religion):**
- *300B (Western Electric)* — eight watts of midrange magic. Low noise,
~40,000-hour life, and a presentation audiophiles describe as simply
*present* — voices and instruments dimensional, on a calm soundstage.
Originals run $6,000–$10,000 a pair; Rossville reissues about $1,500 a
matched pair.
- *2A3 (RCA)* — 3.5 watts, delicate and intimate; the connoisseur's triode.
- *45 (RCA)* — 2 watts of pure midrange; flea-power devotion.
- *845 / 211* — big triodes for powerful SETs: the 300B's drama at
room-filling scale.

**EL84 (Mullard)** — the sweet little pentode: chimey, sparkling, the Vox
AC30's voice. Proof that nine watts can be enough.

## Rectifiers: the feel tube

Often overlooked, never unfelt. The rectifier converts AC to the DC the
amp runs on, and its internal resistance determines **sag** — how the power
supply dips under transient load, subtly compressing dynamics:
- *GZ34 / 5AR4 (Mullard)* — low drop, tight feel; the hi-fi choice.
- *5U4G (RCA)* — more sag, more "bloom" on transients.
- *5Y3* — small amps, maximum sag, vintage looseness.
Silicon diodes don't sag at all — which is precisely, measurably, why
they feel different.

## Buying tubes today

Two markets: **NOS** (new old stock — unused vintage tubes, finite supply,
tested-and-pray pricing, fakes exist, buy from testers with reputations)
and **current production**. The honest landscape:

- **JJ Electronic** (Slovakia) — rugged, affordable, the working musician's
default. Solid, rarely magical.
- **New Sensor** (Russia) — Electro-Harmonix, Sovtek, plus licensed
reissues of Tung-Sol, Mullard, and Genalex Gold Lion. The reissues are
good tubes wearing great names; judge the tube, not the label.
- **Shuguang / Psvane / Linlai** (China) — from workhorse to genuinely
fine 300Bs; the top tiers are serious.
- **Emission Labs, KR Audio, Takatsuki** — boutique, expensive, excellent;
modern engineering aimed at the vintage ideal.
- **Western Electric** (Rossville, GA, USA) — the 300B on original
tooling, and 12AX7-class small-signal tubes announced from 2022.

Always buy **matched pairs/quads** for push-pull output stages, from a
vendor who burns in and tests. A tube is a wear item with a lifespan —
even a 40,000-hour Western Electric eventually goes quiet.

## From glass to DSP: the Analog Warmth experiment

Which brings us to why this primer exists. Kahawai Player is experimenting
with an **Analog Warmth** DSP feature — and this document is its
intellectual honesty policy.

What the tube sound *is*, physically (Section 3), maps surprisingly well
onto things DSP does natively:

- **Harmonic spectrum shaping** — a waveshaper with a soft knee adds
harmonics with a controllable even/odd ratio. "Mostly 2nd harmonic,
decaying with order" is a preset, not a mystery.
- **Soft clipping / gentle compression** — the gradual saturation curve is
trivially modelable, including level-dependent behavior.
- **Transformer-ish bandwidth shaping** — gentle high/low roll-offs and a
touch of phase character approximate the iron's contribution.
- **Sag** — a slow-acting dynamic stage keyed to transient energy mimics
rectifier behavior.

What DSP *cannot* do: put 400 volts across a glowing filament, couple
through a ten-pound transformer, or interact with your speakers'
impedance curve. A model is an *effect inspired by* a circuit, and the
feature will say so — which profiles are measured approximations, which
are artistic interpretations, and what the knobs actually control. No
"virtual Telefunken" claims; the house rule is **never fake a capability**,
and a DSP preset named after a factory it never visited would be exactly
that.

The beautiful lie, labeled as such. That's the most euphonic thing of
all: an honest one.
